Flamingo: Chapter-1

The Last Lesson

Comprehension Questions & Answers

Q.1 What was Franz expected to be prepared with for school that day?

Franz was expected to be prepared with the rules of participles that day as M. Hamel was going to question on participles.

Q.2 What did Franz notice that was unusual about the school that day?

That day the school was so strange and solemn. There was no bustle. Everything was as quiet as Sunday morning. The students were already in their places. These things were so unusual.

Q.3 What had been put up on the bulletin board?

On the bulletin board, there was an order from Berlin to teach only German in the schools of Alsace and Lorraine.

Q.4 What changes did the order from Berlin cause in school that day?

The order from Berlin caused many changes that day in the school as it was the last French lesson. To honour this lesson, M. Hamel wore his embroidered Sunday clothes that he used to wear on special occasions only. The whole school was so strange and solemn, and on the back benches, village people were sitting quietly and in a sad mood.

Q.5 How did Franz’s feelings about M. Hamel and school change?

After acknowledging the order from Berlin and the fact that instead of French, German would be taught in the school, Franz’s feelings changed about M. Hamel and school. He felt sorry for not learning French lessons properly. His books, which seemed to be a nuisance and burden earlier, were now like his old friends. He forgot all about M. Hamel’s ruler and his bad temper and started feeling sorry for him.

Q.6 The people in the story suddenly realise how precious their language is to them. What shows you this? Why does this happen?

The people in the story realise the importance of their language when M. Hamel said that to know and hold fast to their language is like having a key to their prison. This shows that we should love, respect, and guard our own language, culture, tradition, and country.

Q.7 Franz thinks, “Will they make them sing in German, even the pigeons?” What could this mean?

Franz’s thought means that it was so unfair of Germany to impose a foreign language because the mother tongue is a very natural thing, as pigeons have their own natural language and they can’t be forced to sing in German. Besides, this shows a comparison between the people of Alsace and pigeons that pigeons are independent and free to speak their language, but they (people of Alsace) are now slaves and have to adopt a foreign language.

Q.9 How is the mother tongue important to a person? What does M. Hamel, the teacher, say about it?

The mother tongue is the common factor which unites the countrymen. M. Hamel made the villagers realize the importance of the mother tongue. He spoke about the beauty of their mother tongue—the French language. He asked the class to guard it because it was the key to their freedom.

Q.10 How did Franz react to the declaration that it was their last French lesson?

Franz was shocked to know that he could not learn French anymore. He repented that he had not been serious before. He wished he had revised participles and would be able to answer M. Hamel’s questions. Franz gained a sudden liking for his teacher and did not want him to leave.

Q.11 Write the character sketch of M. Hamel.

M. Hamel was a French teacher who had been teaching for forty years in the school of Alsace. He was a strict teacher who always kept an iron ruler under his arm to punish the students. He always thought about the welfare of the children and their better future. He was patriotic and loved his homeland, France. He believed that education plays an important role in preserving one’s language and culture. He loved the French language and considered it the most beautiful language in the world. He earned great respect, as on his last day in the school, many village people came to show their thankfulness.

Q.12 Why was Franz scared that day? What did he see on his way to school and how did he get to his desk?

Franz was not good at learning. He would rather take the day off to waste time searching for birds’ eggs or going sliding on the Saar. Franz was scared that day because M. Hamel had said that he would question them on participles. Franz did not know anything about participles. He found that the day was warm and bright. The birds were chirping at the edge of the woods. The Prussian soldiers were drilling in the open fields. There was a crowd in front of the bulletin-board near the town hall. Franz found the schoolroom unusually quiet. So, he had no option but to open the door and go in before everybody. He blushed and was frightened of the teacher. M. Hamel spoke very kindly to him and asked him to go to his place quickly. Franz jumped over the bench and sat down at his desk.
